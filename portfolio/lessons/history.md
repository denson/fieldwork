# Pueblo History Detective

## Purpose

Practice distinguishing a visible observation, a catalog statement, an inference, and a question that requires another source. Each photo has a notebook and one catalog-based evidence check. The learner may compare all six sources in one exported note.

## 01 — Relief camp

- Library of Congress item: [2017679286](https://www.loc.gov/item/2017679286/).
- Catalog title: A general view of the camp, showing the extent of the tents. Pueblo, Colorado flood.
- Created/published date in the catalog: June 4, 1921.
- A separate note says July 1921 is the date received. Do not substitute it for the created/published date.
- No individual photographer is identified. The caption card or negative sleeve names the source as **Southwestern Division**.
- American National Red Cross photograph collection. Classification: Disaster Relief. Title, date, and notes come from the Red Cross caption card.
- Rights advisory: No known restrictions on publication. [Collection rights information](https://www.loc.gov/rr/print/res/717_anrc.html).

Prompt: “What details suggest how shelter was organized? What other record could tell us whether it was adequate?”

Limits: The photo alone does not establish capacity, the identities of residents, how long they stayed, or whether everyone in Pueblo received shelter. The catalog's title is not proof of those claims.

## 04 — Flood damage

- Library of Congress item: [2017679238](https://www.loc.gov/item/2017679238/).
- Catalog title: View showing the general devastation wrought by the flood to the industrial and business districts Pueblo, Col.
- Catalog date: June 3–4, 1921 **[date received]**. This is not a verified exposure date.
- Photographer or source attribution on the caption card or negative sleeve: Standley.
- American National Red Cross photograph collection. Classification: Disaster Relief. Title, date, and notes come from the Red Cross caption card.
- Rights advisory: No known restrictions on publication.

Prompt: “What details suggest disruption to the city? How do the camp and mess-hall photographs add to—or limit—your explanation of the response?”

Limits: A wide view does not establish total losses, precise water depth, individual experiences, or the fate of particular buildings. The catalog title identifies the subject, while its date field explicitly records receipt.

## 05 — Clothing and supplies

- Library of Congress item: [2017679283](https://www.loc.gov/item/2017679283/).
- Catalog title: A few of the shoes that were sent into the south side Red Cross Relief Station for distribution among the flood sufferers. Pueblo Colorado flood.
- Created/published date in the catalog: June 4, 1921. A separate note records receipt in July 1921.
- No individual photographer identified. Source attribution: Southwest Division.
- American National Red Cross photograph collection. Classification: Disaster Relief. Title, date, and notes come from the Red Cross caption card.
- Rights advisory: No known restrictions on publication.

Prompt: “What needs do the supplies suggest beyond shelter and food? What could a distribution record tell you that this photograph cannot?”

Limits: The title identifies shoes intended for distribution, not a completed distribution. Do not infer the identity or role of the person shown, who received the shoes, or whether everyone received a suitable pair.

## Suggested comparisons

- Damage and response: `flood-damage` with `flood-camp` or `flood-mess-hall`. Connect a visible disruption with a documented form of relief without claiming it met every need.
- Everyday needs: `relief-shoes` with `flood-mess-hall`. Compare material supplies with a food-service setting; ask which records could show actual distribution and coverage.
- Two views from 2015: `steel-mill` with `union-avenue`. Compare subjects and framing by the same photographer without treating either view as a complete portrait of Pueblo.

## Teaching move

When the learner proposes a broad claim, acknowledge the evidence they noticed and ask what part goes beyond the source. A useful case note contains at least one observation, a tentative explanation tied to it, and a specific next source to seek. Do not invent visual details when only the catalog or the learner's note is available.
