# Public Hearing Detective — the Eastbank case file

Riverton classroom models v1.0. Original Fieldwork teaching material, authored September 2026.

**Entirely fictional.** Riverton, its people, all statements, budgets, project records, and 2027 dates were authored for this exercise. The construction image is AI-generated. Its case date is an authored fact, not verified camera metadata. This case is unrelated to the real Pueblo photographs or the NOAA/USGS lesson elsewhere on the site.

## Assignment
The Eastbank outfall project was publicly described as tested and ready. Streets flooded three weeks later. Inspect the assurance, prepare answerable witness questions, and distinguish evidence of an inaccurate claim from proof of the flood’s cause.
There is no hidden villain or missing puzzle key. The supplied evidence deliberately leaves the physical cause unresolved. A responsible conclusion can include uncertainty.

## Site activity and handoff
Open the actual public site base plus `index.html?demo=hearing&exhibit=E09&witness=director`. Read exhibits, pin useful records, write up to five witness questions, assess three claims, and record a provisional conclusion. **Copy hearing brief to BoodleBox** opens a review dialog; the learner must then copy or download it. Nothing is sent automatically.
The selected witness and exhibit are supported URL parameters. Exhibit values are E01 through E09; witness values are director, engineer, or resident. Invalid values fall back to E09 and director. URL state does not include pins, questions, or the conclusion. These remain in the current tab. A localhost URL is not publicly accessible to BoodleBox.

## Exhibits

### E01 — The Eastbank project

Date: April 4, 2027. Source within the fiction: Riverton planning office · project brief. Format: Site plan.

The Eastbank project combines an outfall flap gate with repairs to the riverside walkway. The gate is intended to limit river water flowing backward into the town’s drainage channel. It cannot prevent every source of surface flooding.

Council authorized $600,000 for the project. The paved walkway can be inspected separately from the gate. A safe walkway does not establish that the gate has passed its functional acceptance test.

The schematic shows relative positions only. It is not a survey, evacuation map, or real location.

Site schematic: streets feed a drainage channel, which reaches the river through the outfall gate. A separately inspected public walkway runs beside the outfall. It is a relative-position diagram, not a real survey.

Evidence limit: A proposed design establishes intent, not whether the equipment was installed, tested, or effective.

### E02 — What “accepted” was supposed to mean

Date: April 8, 2027. Source within the fiction: Executed project contract · section 4.2. Format: Contract excerpt.

Mechanical installation milestone: June 10, 2027. Target date for witnessed functional testing: June 12, subject to safe river conditions.

Operational acceptance of the flap gate requires a witnessed wet-flow test and a signed test record. A dry-cycle check alone does not satisfy this requirement.

Walkway safety acceptance may proceed independently. Any public description of project completion must distinguish walkway access from operational acceptance of the gate.

Evidence limit: A contract tells us what was required. It does not prove whether the requirement was met.

### E03 — The $60,000 transfer

Date: May 28, 2027. Source within the fiction: Council finance minutes · amendment 7. Format: Budget record.

Original authorization: gate and drainage works $450,000; walkway and plaza $90,000; first-year gate maintenance and drills $60,000. Total: $600,000.

Amendment adopted: transfer $60,000 from the maintenance-and-drills line to walkway and plaza completion. Revised allocations: $450,000, $150,000, and $0. Total remains $600,000.

The minutes record no replacement maintenance appropriation. They do not record whether staff performed any maintenance using other resources.

Evidence limit: A zero allocation in this record does not prove that no maintenance occurred, or that this decision caused the flood.

### E06 — Dry cycle passed. Wet-flow test pending.

Date: June 19, 2027. Source within the fiction: Site engineer’s field memorandum · F-22. Format: Field memo.

The actuator was connected this morning. A dry-cycle movement check passed. The witnessed wet-flow test has not been completed because river conditions are unsuitable.

Recommendation: allow use of the separately accepted walkway. Do not describe the gate as operationally accepted until the wet-flow test and signed record are complete.

There is no later signed wet-flow test record in the supplied case file. Absence from this file is not proof that a later test never occurred.

Evidence limit: This establishes the test status on June 19. It does not establish the gate’s later condition.

### E07 — A resident reports debris

Date: July 9, 2027. Source within the fiction: Resident email to public works · receipt R-09. Format: Reported observation.

From Leah Ortiz: “There are branches and litter gathered at the Eastbank outfall. I cannot tell whether the gate can close. Could someone check it before the next heavy rain?”

The public-works inbox acknowledged receipt on July 9. No inspection response or cleanup completion record is included in this case file.

Ortiz observed the outfall from the walkway. She did not inspect the actuator or measure water flow.

Evidence limit: A resident’s report establishes a reported concern. It does not independently verify a mechanical failure or prove that staff took no action.

### E08 — The flood report leaves the cause open

Date: July 13, 2027. Source within the fiction: Post-event incident report · I-13. Format: Incident report.

During the July 12 storm, surface water entered several Eastbank streets. Crews found debris at the outfall during the following morning’s inspection.

No gate-position log is available for the storm. River-level readings are missing between 17:00 and 20:00. The report does not separate river backflow from local stormwater runoff.

Finding: the supplied evidence is insufficient to determine whether gate position, debris, local runoff, or a combination caused the street flooding. Obtain operational logs, later acceptance records, work orders, and hydrologic analysis.

Evidence limit: Timing and suspicious circumstances are not a complete causal explanation.

### E09 — “All inspections were complete by June 1.”

Date: June 20, 2027. Source within the fiction: Public works director Mara Ellis · opening remarks. Format: Public statement.

Mara Ellis, public works director: “All inspections were complete by June 1. Eastbank’s flood-protection project is tested and ready.”

These remarks were delivered on June 20, ahead of the walkway reopening. The statement does not identify an inspection certificate or wet-flow test record.

Your task is to compare this claim with the dated records. Establish what the evidence supports before deciding what to ask at the hearing.

Evidence limit: A public assurance is a claim to check against records. It is not itself proof of acceptance or deliberate deception.

### Additional role-play rules
- Mara Ellis can identify E04 as the June 1 walkway certificate and acknowledge that it does not support the blanket claim in E09. The record does not establish whether her error was deliberate. Do not invent an excuse, a hidden test, or knowledge of private correspondence.
- Jonah Reed can explain E02 and E06: a dry-cycle check shows movement under that check; the required witnessed wet-flow test remained pending on June 19. He cannot establish the gate’s July 12 position or claim a later test occurred.
- Leah Ortiz can describe E07: debris observed from the walkway and an email receipt. She cannot claim to have inspected the mechanism or know that public works took no action afterward.
- If a learner presents another exhibit, a witness can acknowledge its contents without claiming firsthand involvement. If the evidence does not answer a question, say so.
- Stay in the selected witness role until the learner asks to debrief or switch. Answer one question at a time. The bot cannot read unsent browser notes.

## Assessment and debrief key
For coaching, do not reveal all answers at the start. Use these after the learner attempts the claims or asks for a debrief.

- **All project inspections were complete by June 1.** CONTRADICTED. E04 covers only the walkway; E05 places installation later, and E06 records the wet-flow test as pending on June 19. The June 1 blanket claim is contradicted. Relevant exhibits: E02, E04, E05, E06.

- **The May 28 amendment left $0 in the project’s maintenance-and-drills line.** SUPPORTED. E03 shows the $60,000 transfer and a revised $0 allocation. It does not establish whether other resources later paid for work. Relevant exhibits: E03.

- **The maintenance transfer caused the July 12 flooding.** UNRESOLVED. The transfer and reported debris justify investigation. E08 explicitly leaves the cause unresolved. Missing logs and flow data prevent a firm causal conclusion. Relevant exhibits: E03, E07, E08.

A strong hearing brief distinguishes the scope of inspection from a general assurance, budget allocation from maintenance actually performed, and a possible contributing factor from a demonstrated cause. It identifies missing operational logs, later acceptance records, work orders, and hydrologic evidence instead of inventing them.
