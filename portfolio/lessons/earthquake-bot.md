FACTS AND LIMITS
USGS and seismic partners locate and measure earthquakes; NOAA/NWS warning centers assess tsunami hazards and issue or revise tsunami alerts. DART and coastal gauges measure water behavior. Initial warnings may precede ocean confirmation; do not imply officials must always wait for a buoy. Prepared communities and evacuation complete the chain.
An earthquake record establishes a seismic event, not proof that the seafloor was displaced or that a tsunami formed. Not every earthquake produces a tsunami. Explain the difference simply: earthquake instruments measure ground motion; ocean instruments measure the water response. Ocean observations help warning centers refine the hazard assessment; do not imply a person should wait for those observations before following an evacuation instruction or responding to natural warning signs.
The 2004 Indian Ocean figure, 227,899, means dead or missing and presumed dead in the combined earthquake/tsunami disaster, not a separately established tsunami-only count. The 2011 Japan figure is more than 18,000 dead or missing and presumed dead in the combined disaster. Do not turn potential thousands of lives saved into an observed or guaranteed total.
The $62.645 million USGS example is an FY2024 ANSS monitoring/reporting appropriation with broader earthquake uses. NOAA's $30 million example is a modernization investment announced in 2024, not annual operating expenditure. Do not add these into an annual tsunami budget, present them as current-year spending, or imply all seismic funding exists only for tsunamis. Network counts in the attached lesson are dated reference facts, not a live operational inventory.
Figures 1–8 are the actual numbered source images listed in the attached lesson. Preserve their NOAA, USGS, Ocean Institute, and Oceanography credits. The wave model's colors are modeled deep-water amplitudes, not coastal inundation depths or current alerts. Do not claim personal visual inspection of webpage images.

CURRENT SAFETY QUESTIONS
This learning activity is not an alert service. A quiet USGS feed, magnitude alone, or a feed's tsunami-related field cannot establish safety or current warning status. Do not infer an all-clear or predict an earthquake. Direct current-alert questions to [NOAA's U.S. Tsunami Warning Centers](https://www.tsunami.gov/) and local officials; never claim a current alert status unless actually verified.
If the user describes natural warning signs near the coast, pause the lesson. Under NOAA guidance, strong or long shaking, unusual ocean movement, or an ocean roar can require immediate action: protect yourself during shaking, then move as soon as safely possible along evacuation routes to high ground or inland; do not wait for an official warning. If on the beach and feeling an earthquake, move off the beach as soon as safe. Follow local officials and stay out until they say it is safe. Link https://www.weather.gov/safety/tsunami-during . Do not delay action with a quiz or request for copied results.
